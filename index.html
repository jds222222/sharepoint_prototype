Text · 2 columns

What Copilot is best for, and what it's not for

Copilot is a strong first-draft and catch-up assistant. It doesn't replace your judgement or Carrier's systems of record.

Best for

  • Catching up: summarizing long email threads, chats and meetings
  • First drafts of emails, documents and presentations
  • Finding information across the files and messages you can access
  • Rewriting, shortening or changing the tone of a text
  • Exploring data in Excel and suggesting formulas
  • Brainstorming ideas and options

Not for

  • Final decisions without a human review
  • Figures, legal or customer-facing content you haven't checked
  • Sharing data you aren't allowed to share
  • Replacing Carrier's official systems and sources of truth
  • Personal or HR judgements about colleagues

Source: Microsoft Copilot FAQ, Carrier acceptable use policy [to validate]

How a good prompt is built

A clear prompt gives Copilot four things. You don't always need all four, but the more you give, the better the answer.

1 · Goal

What do you want? "Summarize", "draft", "compare".

2 · Context

Why you need it and who it's for.

3 · Source

Which file, email or meeting to use. Type / to point to one.

4 · Expectations

Format, length and tone of the answer.

Source: Microsoft, "Write effective prompts" · Viva Learning, "The art of prompting"

Text · 2 columns

Before and after

Before
Write a summary of the project.

Copilot has to guess which project, for whom, and how long.

After
Summarize /[Project update.docx] for my director. Focus on budget and timeline risks. Keep it to 3 bullet points.

Goal, source, context and expectations are all there.

Tips to get better answers

Be specific about what you want

"Draft a 3-line reply that accepts the meeting and asks for the agenda" works better than "reply to this".

Point Copilot to the right source

Type / and pick the file, email or meeting. Copilot then works from that content instead of searching widely.

Keep the conversation going

The first answer is a draft. Ask follow-ups: "shorter", "more formal", "add a table", "what's missing?".

Ask for the format you need

Bullet points, a table, an email, a list of actions with owners: say it in the prompt.

Ask Copilot to check its work

"Which parts of this are you unsure about?" or "show me where this comes from" helps you review faster.

Do you need an agent?

An assistant answers. An agent finishes the work. Build only when an agent earns its keep.

Build an agent when

  • The work is repeatable and many people will use it
  • Someone owns it after launch
  • The process is clearly defined
  • The data it needs is approved for use

Use Copilot Chat instead when

  • It's a one-off question
  • It's about your personal files
  • Nobody has defined the process yet

Source: Carrier D30 deck, "AI Platforms 2.0: Building Agents", slide 5

Why Copilot can get it wrong

Copilot predicts the most likely answer from what it can see. If a source is missing, outdated or unclear, it can fill the gap with something that sounds right but isn't. This is called a hallucination.

Source: Microsoft Learn, validate Copilot output

Open the sources
